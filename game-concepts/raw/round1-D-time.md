# D. Time designer: four concepts

---

## D1. Proof

**One sentence:** You run a one-oven sourdough bakery where the starter and the doughs ferment in real time, and you use feeding ratios, warmth and the fridge to make each thing ripen when you'll actually be looking.

**30 seconds of play**
It's 07:48. The app opens on a counter seen from above. The starter jar's rubber band marks last night's level. It has risen to 2.4× and the dome is just starting to flatten, so it has peaked. You drag three spoons into a clean jar: one of starter, five of flour, five of water (1:5:5). An arc appears under it: *peak ~17:30*. You pour the rest into the big bowl and mix by circling your thumb. Then you slide the bowl along the counter. The window end is 19°C and the oven end is 26°C, and as you drag, the "ready" arc moves from 15:10 to 11:40. You stop it at 13:00, which is your lunch break. The order board shows *Okafor, 2 loaves, pickup 18:00*. You close the app.

**Central decision and response**
*When do I want to be back?* Every lever sets a time in the future, so you're really scheduling your own return. Dough has a window of about ±45 minutes rather than an exact moment. After baking, each loaf is sliced on screen. Underproofed loaves are dense, well-timed ones have an open, irregular crumb, and overproofed ones spread flat. Customers pay according to the crumb. Optional stretch-and-folds (swipe the four sides of the dough) make a taller loaf if they're spaced at least 30 minutes apart.

**Concrete interaction / surprise**
At 22:10 you shape two loaves and put them in the fridge. The cold slows them to about ⅛ speed, so their window stretches to 07:00–13:00 tomorrow. On Wednesday you forget a bowl at the warm end. At 15:20 it has tripled and collapsed. The game doesn't bin it. It offers a rescue card: press it into a tray as focaccia and sell it at half price. Mrs. Okafor's grandson loves it and places a standing Saturday order. The starter also has a memory. Feed it daily for 14 days and it develops a named trait ("tangy", "rye-hungry") that unlocks a recipe. Skip three days and grey hooch forms on top. You pour it off and feed twice to revive it, and it loses one trait step.

**Between visits / reason to return**
New orders arrive overnight with pickup times spread across tomorrow, and you accept the ones that fit your own day ("meetings 10–14, no noon pickups"). New flours change the speeds: rye is about 30% faster. Something on the counter is always moving toward its window.

**Borrowed logic:** Home sourdough baking, where feeding ratios and cold retarding are used to bend fermentation around the baker's sleep and work hours.

## D2. Radio Sked

**One sentence:** You lead a small Himalayan expedition from base camp over five to nine real days, and you can only talk to your climbers at radio calls you schedule in advance. Between calls, they act on their own.

**30 seconds of play**
At 12:00 the phone buzzes once for the call you booked. Static hisses, and you hold the talk button. *"Dawa: Camp 3. Wind 45. Priya coughing since 09:00. Over."* Behind the message is a side profile of the mountain with three climber dots and their oxygen counts, plus a 48-hour weather strip with a pale gap on Thursday from 02:00 to 13:00. You drag response cards onto the climbers. Priya gets *Descend to C2 with Dawa*. Mira gets *Hold C3; push to C4 tomorrow if wind under 30*. You drag a pin on a 24-hour ring to set the next call for 18:30, then let go. "Base out."

**Central decision and response**
You decide what to say and how long until you speak again. Longer gaps give the climbers more autonomy, and they read standing orders through their personalities. Mira is summit-hungry and reads "under 30" generously. Dawa is a cautious veteran. Miss a call, and they follow the missed-call protocol you chose at the start. At the next call the game answers with their logs, such as *"06:00 wind 32. Mira left for C4 anyway."* Climbers must also climb high and sleep low over several real hours to acclimatize, and skipping those rotations raises the risk of altitude illness.

**Concrete interaction / surprise**
Summit day. The night before, you set a turnaround time of 11:00. At your 11:40 call, Mira is 80 m below the top with her bottle at 30%: *"I can see it. Forty minutes."* Wind is forecast to rise after 14:00. Fourteen other headlamps are on the ridge, because other players' expeditions chose the same Thursday window and the queue at the rock step is adding 50 minutes. If you say "go," she summits at 12:25, and you then pick 13:30 or 14:00 for the next call, knowing she'll descend into the wind on your last words. If you hold the turnaround, her trust drops by 1, but you get a second window in three days if the oxygen lasts.

**Between visits / reason to return**
The expedition persists. Climbers remember broken promises, and frostbite carries into future seasons. Later mountains change the radio logistics, such as a ridge with no relay coverage above 7,000 m. Someone is waiting at the time you said you'd call.

**Borrowed logic:** High-altitude mountaineering practice: fixed radio schedules, climb-high-sleep-low acclimatization and a turnaround time committed to in advance.

## D3. Statement

**One sentence:** Once a day you get one four-second look at a busy street corner, and over the following hours an investigator questions you about it. The longer you wait to answer, the more each correct answer is worth.

**30 seconds of play**
It's 08:14 at a bus stop. You tap **Look** and the corner plays for four seconds: a tram crosses, a woman in a yellow raincoat drops something, a man pays for a newspaper with coins, a red car pulls in. Only a small circle under your thumb is sharp, and you slide it to choose what to see clearly. Then the scene fades into a pencil outline of blank shapes. At 11:30 a notebook page opens: *"What did the woman in the yellow raincoat drop?"* You tap *glove* and stack 4 of your 5 confidence chips on it. Because you're right, the glove gets drawn into the outline.

**Central decision and response**
There are two decisions: where to look during the four seconds, and when to answer. Questions unlock one per hour and can be answered any time before midnight. The multiplier is 1× right away, 2× after 4 hours and 3× after 9 hours, so waiting is a bet on your own memory. A correct answer scores its chips × the multiplier, and a wrong one loses the chips. The sketch fills in with what you got right, and wrong answers are drawn in red, so your memory sits next to what really happened.

**Concrete interaction / surprise**
At 16:00 the question is *"How fast was the red car going when it hit the bin?"* (10 / 20 / 30 km/h). The car parked without hitting anything. A small **Challenge the question** button pays 5× if you use it correctly. Cases run all week at the same corner. On Tuesday the investigator casually mentions "the newsagent's umbrella," which didn't exist. On Thursday he asks its colour, and naming one means the umbrella was planted in your memory. On Friday the bakery till is stolen between glimpses: *"On which day was the man in the grey cap here, and what was he carrying?"* Now it matters that on Monday your focus was on the newspaper stand.

**Between visits / reason to return**
There's a new glimpse every day and a case every week. If your statement convicts the wrong man, next week opens with his appeal. A witness profile tracks what you tend to notice (faces, hands, colours, motion), and the questions start aiming at your blind spots. Optionally, everyone sees the same corner, and a 21:00 "line-up" shows what percentage of players remembered each detail.

**Borrowed logic:** Police eyewitness interviews and the scouts' Kim's Game: a fixed viewing time, delayed recall, and leading questions that can plant details that were never there.

## D4. Held Note (strange one)

**One sentence:** Each time you check your phone you choose one note, and it keeps sounding until your next check. The gaps in your day become the rhythm, and the day loops every 24 hours as music layered over your previous days.

**30 seconds of play**
*Assume a lock-screen widget, so changing the note takes one tap and no unlock.*
It's 10:47 and you're waiting for the kettle. The widget shows a clock-like ring with coloured arcs for today's notes and fainter arcs for the past six days. The playhead is at 10:47, and your current note, an E started at 10:12, glows. You tap **listen** and the loop plays at one real hour per second, so the whole day passes in 24 seconds. This morning's E sits comfortably over Monday's C. Ahead on the ring, Tuesday's layer switches to F at 13:00. You tap a pitch pad for G. The E arc closes and a G arc begins, and the phone goes back in your pocket.

**Central decision and response**
*Which pitch fits what the old layers will play while I'm gone?* Your note lasts until you return, so choosing one means predicting your own absence. G suits Monday's C but grinds against Tuesday's F after 13:00, so a meeting that runs until 14:30 means 90 minutes of clash. Agreeing arcs glow and ring brightly in playback, while clashing arcs fray and audibly beat. A **rest** pad holds silence instead. At midnight the day is pressed into the loop as a layer. The loop keeps seven layers, each losing 15% volume per day like worn tape.

**Concrete interaction / surprise**
At 23:51 you pick a low A and fall asleep. It holds until 07:05, a seven-hour drone. After a week, the night side of the ring is a thick chord built from seven bedtime choices. On Saturday you sleep until 10:40, which leaves a gap where your weekday commute checks (07:30, 07:34, 07:41, 07:52) had made a quick bright figure. **Duets:** a friend's loop can overlay yours, and when you both check at 12:30 lunch, your note changes land together and the ring marks a *cadence*. **Prompts:** *"Play the opening of 'Frère Jacques' (C D E C) between 12:00 and 13:00,"* which takes four well-spaced checks in one hour.

**Between visits / reason to return**
Every check rewrites the piece, and each full week unlocks a new scale or timbre (kalimba, reed organ). You can export "your week" as 2:48 of audio (7 × 24 seconds). The note you're holding is still sounding, and the ring shows exactly when it will start to clash.

**Borrowed logic:** Live looping with a loop pedal (a fixed-length cycle, recording only at the playhead, each pass overdubbed on earlier ones), plus the gradual wear of a tape loop.
