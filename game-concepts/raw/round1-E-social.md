# E. Social designer: four concepts

---

## E1. Pace Notes

**1. One sentence.** Two people form a rally crew: one drives a stage slowly in clear weather writing compressed pace notes, then reads them aloud while the other races the same stage flat out in fog.

**2. 30 seconds of play**
- *Recce (co-driver alone).* Sam sees the stage in daylight, with the car capped at 50 km/h. As each feature approaches, Sam taps a palette along the bottom of the screen: L/R, then severity 1–6 (1 = hairpin, 6 = nearly flat), then optional modifiers ("tightens", "crest", "don't cut", "caution") and a distance (30/50/100/150). The notebook holds only 40 lines per stage.
- *Stage (together, on a sofa or a call).* Priya's screen shows 20 m of gravel in fog. Her left thumb steers and her right works the throttle. Sam's screen shows no road, only the scrolling notebook and a trip meter. Sam reads: "Hundred… left four… into right three tightens… don't cut." Priya brakes before she sees anything. The right-hander comes out of the fog where it was promised. She holds the outside, away from the rock sitting on the inside line. Stage time: 2:14.6.

**3. Central decision and response.** The co-driver decides what to leave out and which number to give. The driver decides how far to trust a number she can't check yet. After each stage the game shows a calibration strip: "Sam's 4s: taken at 82, 79, 91 km/h. Possible: 95." Over a few sessions the pair learns that Sam's 4 is Priya's 5. Then Sam either re-grades, or Priya learns to translate. The notebook's language belongs to the pair.

**4. Interaction / surprise.** The recce was dry; the stage starts in rain. Sam gets one "minus one" call per stage, which drops every following number by one until Sam taps "back to notes." On stage 4, Sam calls "minus one" after Priya slides wide on an L5. Sam forgets to cancel it when the road dries at km 3.2, so Priya brakes for six corners she could have taken flat and loses 4.1 s. Each season the pair can also add one custom word to their palette (for example, "Nan's" = "a 3 that looks like a 5"). On monthly Swap Notes stages you drive from another crew's notebook, and their "caution" turns out to mean your "don't cut".

**5. Between rounds / return.** Six new stages arrive each weekend (gravel, snow, night tarmac), identical for every crew, with times on a board. Roles swap each stage, so each person learns to write for the other. The shared notebook is the reason to come back as a pair.

**6. Borrowed logic:** Rally co-driving: the speed-limited recce, the numbered severity scale with modifiers, and notes read against the trip meter to a driver who hasn't seen the road yet.

## E2. Kasen

**1. One sentence.** Friends who live far apart build a 36-photo chain over a few weeks, where each photo must link to the previous one and turn away from the one before that.

**2. 30 seconds of play.** It's 8:10 a.m. in Lisbon, and Mara's notification reads "Your verse. 11 of 36." Jonah's photo fills the screen: a laundromat dryer with one red sock pressed to the glass, and his link word, "turning." Under it, faded to 30%, is the verse before: Mara's own Ferris wheel, link word "round." The rule bar says *link to the dryer · step away from the wheel.* "Turning" was the last link and can't be used again, and anything round would echo the wheel. So she picks up the sock. On her walk she sees a red postbox and takes the photo with the in-app camera (no gallery, so every photo is from today). She types "red." The scroll animates wheel → dryer → postbox, with the link words between them like hinges. Sent.

**3. Central decision and response.** The decision is which part of your friend's photo to pick up, and how far to step away. A near link (another sock) is easy but flat. A far link ("waiting," because the sock looks stuck) takes the chain somewhere new. The receiver answers with one tap:
- **Linked** accepts the photo.
- **Backstep** says it echoes the faded verse. Each player may call one retake per chain.
- **Leap** marks a link the receiver admires, with a brushstroke on the scroll.

**4. Interaction / surprise.** Verses 5, 13 and 29 are moon seats; 17 and 35 are blossom seats. Mara's verse 13 falls on a night with a 92% waxing gibbous, and she shoots it over the river at 22:40. Jonah, five hours behind, links verse 14 with the word "same" and shoots that moon over Lake Ontario. The scroll draws a thin arc between the two photos. If cloud hides the moon, anything round and lit counts as a stand-in (a streetlamp, a fried egg), but it doesn't get the gold seal.

**5. Between rounds / return.** The opening photo must show the opener's real season, so October chains feel different from April ones. A finished chain prints as a scroll: "Teacup to car wash in 36 steps · 4 leaps · 1 backstep." The *sabaki* role, which can ask for one rewrite, rotates each chain. People come back because a friend is waiting and the day has become a hunt for something red.

**6. Borrowed logic:** Japanese linked verse in its 36-verse *kasen* form: each verse links to the last and shifts away from the one before it, moon and blossom verses have fixed seats, and a master may ask for a rewrite.

## E3. Long Hum (strange one)

**1. One sentence.** A family or a group of friends, together or spread across cities, keep one shared hum going without a break for as long as they can, each humming into their own phone, and nobody can hold a note forever.

*Assumption:* each phone detects whether its own player is voicing and streams the hum to the others' earbuds with about 150 ms of delay. Breaks are judged from each phone's own timestamps, so the delay changes what you hear, not the score.

**2. 30 seconds of play.** Sunday, 9 p.m. Gran is in Cork, Theo and Ruth in London, and Ash in Melbourne at breakfast, each with one earbud in. The screen is black except for a thread spinning left to right, as thick as the number of people humming. Gran starts: a thin gold line. Theo slides in softly and the line doubles, then Ruth, then Ash. At 0:19 Gran runs low and fades out over a second, which leaves a smooth strand end. Ruth is low too, but she heard Gran leave, so she holds two more seconds before fading. Two strands remain. Gran slips back in. Nobody has spoken.

**3. Central decision and response.** The decision is when to breathe. You can leave early while others are strong, or hold on and risk running dry at the same moment as everyone else. Every exit and entry is audible. The thread records each one:
- A fade of 1 s or more leaves a smooth end.
- A sudden stop frays the end and costs 3 s of length.
- A loud entry ties a knot.
- If every strand ends at once, the thread snaps and its length shows: 1:47.

**4. Interaction / surprise.** At set times the thread must pass through a *needle*: every voice at once for 3 s. A needle appears at 1:30 with a 10-second warning. New groups all fill their lungs, hum through it, and are all empty at 1:34, so the thread snaps. Then they learn the counter-move. In this family it's Ash: he breathes on purpose at 1:25, re-enters at 1:29, and carries the thread alone at 1:35 while the others gasp. In *chord rounds*, each player gets a reference note (root, third, fifth or octave). The thread is gold only while all four sound.

**5. Between rounds / return.** Each session's thread is woven as one row of the group's cloth, coloured by the chords held, so after a few months a family has a textile only they made. In *Bedtime mode* for two, the thread is a candle, and the screen goes dark when it snaps. *Strangers' hum* matches you with three random voices for one round, with no chat; the thread is the only record.

**6. Borrowed logic:** Choral staggered breathing: singers sustain a line longer than any one breath by never breathing at the same moment as their neighbour, tapering out and slipping back in softly so the sound never breaks.

## E4. You Cut, I Choose

**1. One sentence.** Two siblings (or housemates) split hauls of odd objects by the kitchen rule that one cuts and the other chooses, while each secretly collects something different, so every cut is a guess about what the other wants.

**2. 30 seconds of play.** Round 3 is "Grandad's shed." Eight items sit on a wooden table on both phones: a jar of screws, a blue kite, a cracked compass, a button tin, a fishing float, a brass key, half a chocolate bar and a cassette labelled "SUMMER 87." Ana is cutting. She drags a finger across the table, and items snap to either side of the line. The chocolate is the only item she can divide, so she pinches the line through it and slides it to 60/40. She seals the cut. On Leo's phone the two piles slide apart. He looks for four seconds and taps the left one. It flies to his shelf, and the other pile goes to hers. Next round, Leo cuts. Each move takes 10–30 seconds, so a game can also be played asynchronously over a day.

**3. Central decision and response.** Each player has a hidden card:
- **Ana: "Summer things."** Cassette 5, kite 4, float 3.
- **Leo: "Things that open."** Key 5, compass 4, tin 3, jar 2.
- **Both cards:** 1 point for every 10% of the chocolate.

The cutter must make two piles she'd be equally happy to get, because the chooser picks first. She can also bait him by putting what she thinks he wants beside junk she doesn't mind losing. The chooser weighs what he wants against what keeps the most from her.

**4. Interaction / surprise.** House rule: if both cards are complete by round 8, it's *dessert*, and both scores double. That puts a price on spite. At the end both cards flip, and the game replays every move with tags. *"Leo took the cassette three times. Worth 0 to him, 5 to you. Spite ×3."* *"Ana put the key where you'd surely pick it, twice. Gift ×2."* They missed dessert by one cassette. *Heirloom* items return every game. The brass key keeps a history: "Lived with Ana 14 times, Leo 3." Whoever holds it at the end opens the locked-trunk haul next game, which contains one item only their card can use.

**5. Between rounds / return.** Each game runs 8 rounds. Hauls rotate (attic, fridge, toy box, holiday leftovers), and so do the cards. A lifetime sibling ledger of gifts, spites and heirloom custody keeps growing. A three-player variant uses *last diminisher*: each player in turn may trim the pile on offer, and the last one to trim must take it.

**6. Borrowed logic:** "You cut, I choose" as siblings do it over cake: the cutter divides by her own values because the other person picks first, so every cut is both honest and tactical.
