# Round 1: A. Interaction inventor

---

## A1. Strung

**Your fingertips are the pegs of a string figure: with the phone flat on a table, you hook, drop and swap loops across up to six fingers, and the figure only shows itself when you spread your hands.**

*Assume the phone tracks six simultaneous touches; small screens get four-peg figures.*

**30 seconds of play**
- **0–3 s:** Put down thumbs and little fingers, and a wool loop snaps around them. Each strand's pitch rises as you spread.
- **3–10 s:** Slide your right index finger in from the edge. **Sliding passes under strands, and lifting and tapping down beyond a strand passes over it.** Rest on the left palm string for 0.3 s and it catches. Mirror it with your left index.
- **10–20 s:** The target card shows four diamonds. Lift both thumbs and their loops zip off. Hop the thumbs over the near strings and slide them under the far ones. A thumb now holds two loops, so double-tap it to "Navajo": the lower loop lifts over the upper one and off.
- **20–30 s:** Spread all six fingers slowly. The strands slide, climb a scale and lock. A correct figure lands on a clean chord with a click. A wrong one buzzes into a tangle.

**Central decision**
Which strand, which finger, under or over. Lifting a finger drops everything on it, with no undo. As you extend, the pitch tells you whether the figure is right before you see it.

**Interesting interaction**
**Cat's Cradle on one screen.** Player 1 holds the Cradle on four fingers. Player 2 pinches the two X crossings, pulls them past the side strings and swoops up through the middle. Player 1 lifts within 0.25 s and the figure becomes Soldier's Bed on Player 2's fingers. Lift 0.4 s early and it collapses into a lump.

**The string's material changes the rules.**
- **Silk:** if a finger with a loop drifts 5 mm, the loop slides off.
- **Elastic:** a released loop snaps back and can catch a neighbouring finger, giving you a loop you never planned.

**Between rounds / reason to return**
- A figure book runs from 3 to 20 moves, each on assigned string, including beaded cord whose beads must finish on set crossings.
- The daily figure is shown only as a silhouette, so you work the moves out backwards.
- Figures you invent can be named and sent to friends as silhouettes.

**Borrowed logic:** String figures. Loops live on fingers, string can't be cut or undone, the Navajo move lifts a lower loop over an upper one, and the figure appears only on extension.

## A2. Gather

**Blow into the mic to inflate molten glass while your thumb keeps the pipe rolling, and steer the shape by choosing which parts to cool before each breath.**

*Assume the mic can tell a steady breath from speech by its broadband rush.*

**30 seconds of play**
- **0–4 s:** In landscape, tip the phone forward to dip the pipe in the furnace. Your left thumb swipes along the pipe to roll it, winding on honey-thick glass.
- **4–8 s:** A fist-sized gather glows yellow-orange at 1,120 °C and slumps toward the real floor. Keep rolling or it droops off-centre.
- **8–12 s:** Drag it across the steel marver. It sizzles, and the touched face dulls to orange, now stiffer than the rest.
- **12–18 s:** Raise the phone and puff once, and a bubble opens. Cap the mouthpiece with your thumb and the trapped air heats and keeps swelling the bubble.
- **18–24 s:** The lip fades to dull red. Three seconds in the glory hole brightens it; any longer and it drips.
- **24–30 s:** Pinch with the jacks while rolling to cut a neck. Tip the phone nose-down and the bubble stretches into a teardrop.

**Central decision**
You decide where the glass is cold and where it's hot before each breath, and when to reheat instead of shape. **Breath always expands the hottest, thinnest part.** With no heat map, you read colour and sound: hot glass hisses, cool glass clicks.

**Interesting interaction**
**The order is a bowl 16 cm wide and 5 cm deep.**
1. Marver the bottom for 2 s so it drops to about 750 °C.
2. Hold the gather half into the glory hole so only the sides reheat.
3. Blow. The stiff base holds while the sides balloon into a wide, flat form.
4. Attach a punty rod, crack off the pipe, and spin the jacks inside the lip so the rim flares open.

**The annealer reveals it next session.** A 9 mm base with 2 mm walls greets you with a *ping* and a crack. Even 3–4 mm walls survive.

**Between rounds / reason to return**
- An order book of silhouettes with tolerances.
- Colour cane to twist into spirals, plus new tools (paddles, blocks, steaming wet newspaper).
- Each session opens with last night's annealer. Survivors ship, and cracked pieces are remelted.
- Two-player mode: one person blows while the other rolls and shapes.

**Borrowed logic:** Glassblowing. Glass inflates where it's hottest, so you shape it by cooling selectively. It has to be rolled constantly against gravity, and uneven thickness cracks in the annealer.

## A3. Belfry

**You ring one tower bell in a band of six by pulling its rope, and because each pull decides when the bell sounds on the *next* stroke, you change your place in the order by planning one swing ahead.**

**30 seconds of play**
- **0 s:** A rope hangs down the screen with a striped sally at thumb height. You are bell 3 of 6, ringing rounds: 1-2-3-4-5-6.
- **2 s:** Drag the sally down and the rope shoots up. The bell swings over, and half a second later it strikes with a haptic thud.
- **4 s:** The tail end comes down. Drag it for the backstroke.
- **6 s:** The conductor calls "Go Plain Hunt." Now you ring 4th, 5th, 6th twice, then back down to 1st twice, and up again. A row takes about 2 s, so one place is about 0.3 s.
- **6–30 s:** To ring later, pull long, and the bell rises near balance and hangs. To ring earlier, check it with a 60% pull so it returns sooner. You choose one stroke before you hear the result. Two bells together clang, and good rows tick evenly.

**Central decision**
How long to pull each stroke so the bell lands in the right slot next row, while tracking your place in the method. Striking is scored in milliseconds. Your path (the "blue line") can be shown or hidden. Computer ringers have habits, such as bell 5 rushing, that you adjust around.

**Interesting interaction**
**A call.** A Plain Bob Minor plain course runs 60 rows, about two minutes. Mid-course the conductor shouts "Bob!" Your bell was about to dodge down from 4ths, but must now ring 4th twice, then turn up. You have one row, and the pull that decides it was made a stroke ago.

**An overpull.** Flick the sally past the bottom edge and the bell swings over balance. The stay snaps, the rope whips off-screen, and the band stands in silence. The correct finish is a gentle hold at the bottom of the backstroke, which sets the bell upright.

**Between rounds / reason to return**
- Methods climb from Plain Hunt to Plain Bob, Grandsire, Stedman and Cambridge Surprise, on 6 to 12 bells.
- Heavier bells answer more slowly: on a 24 cwt tenor, you commit almost a stroke earlier.
- A weekly practice night has live calls, and the long challenge is a quarter peal (1,260 changes, about 45 minutes).
- Your recorded ringing becomes a ghost bell in other players' bands, so six friends can ring a touch together, mistakes and all.

**Borrowed logic:** English change ringing. Bells only swap adjacent places each row, and the bell's swing means you set your timing a stroke ahead by how high you let it rise.

## A4. Pocket Tumbler (strange one)

**Your phone is the barrel of a rock tumbler: you load rough stones and grit, then barely look at it, because your ordinary walking, commuting and fidgeting grind the stones smooth over four stages.**

*Assume the phone can log background motion as walking, running, vehicle vibration or stillness.*

**30 seconds of play (Sunday loading)**
- **0–8 s:** Drag rough stones into a rubber barrel: agates, red jasper, rose quartz, obsidian, sea glass. Each thunk's haptic weight matches its grams.
- **8–14 s:** At 55% full the stones would crash and chip, so add ceramic pellets to reach 70%.
- **14–20 s:** Tilt the phone to pour coarse 60/90 grit (the angle sets the flow), then water to just cover the stones.
- **20–30 s:** Twist the lid with two fingers until it clicks three times. Pocket the phone.

**Check-ins.** Shake the phone twice and **listen**. Angular stones clack sharply, rounded ones *tock*, and spent grit sounds muffled. The screen shows only a closed barrel and a tumble counter.

**Central decision**
What to load together, how long each stage runs, and when to open the barrel.
- **Coarse stage:** about 18,000 tumbles (one step is one tumble, so roughly 3 days). Grit is spent every 7,000, so you open, tilt out the slurry and recharge.
- **Moving on too early** locks in pits permanently. Each extra 1,000 tumbles costs about 1% of the stones' mass.
- **Medium, prepolish and polish:** about 7,000 tumbles each.
- **Rinsing:** before each stage, swirl the phone in circles for 8 s. One coarse grain carried into the polish stage scratches every stone.
- **Motion types:** a bus's vibration polishes gently, while running cuts fast but bruises quartz with white crescents.

**Interesting interaction**
**Mixed hardness.** Obsidian (5.5) tumbled with agate (7): by 10,000 tumbles the obsidian is half its size and frosted, and the agates are barely touched.

**The thunder egg.** A 210 g ugly brown lump. Around 12,000 tumbles your shake picks up a hollow *tink*. Open the barrel and the rind has worn through into a 4 mm window of blue chalcedony lined with crystals. Keep tumbling to widen the window but lose crystals, or pull it and hand-polish the window by rubbing circles on the screen.

**Between rounds / reason to return**
- A cycle takes about a week, and finished stones go on a shelf with their history, such as "mass lost 22%; 64% bus, 31% walking."
- Rough arrives in weekly bags, and a second barrel lets you stagger stages.
- A stone sent to ride in a friend's barrel logs every pocket it travels in.

**Borrowed logic:** Rotary rock tumbling. The grit sequence can't be rushed, fill level and hardness must match, grit from one stage contaminates the next, and you judge progress by the barrel's sound instead of by sight.
